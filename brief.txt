OpenVision – project brief (updated 2026-11-04)
===============================================

GOAL
A camera on my eyeglasses that sends what I see to a local AI model on my Galaxy S24 FE.
The model describes the scene and answers my questions, out loud, in Persian (or English).
Everything runs on the phone, offline after the first model download. No cloud.

CURRENT PROTOTYPE (index.html, runs in Chrome on the phone)
- Models run in the browser through WebGPU (transformers.js): Qwen3-VL 2B (recommended),
  Qwen3.5 0.8B (lightest), Gemma 4 E2B / E4B (E4B often runs out of memory).
- No live video. The camera opens only for a moment to grab one frame, then closes
  before the model runs (camera + model competing for GPU memory caused crashes).
- ONE BUTTON:
    * Tap        → capture a frame → describe it in 1–2 sentences → read it aloud.
    * Long press → the frame is captured at the START of the press → microphone opens
                   to ask a question about that frame; a text box appears too, for typing.
                   If nothing is heard, the text box stays open.
    * Space / Enter on a Bluetooth keyboard-mode button does the same (for a future
      physical button on the glasses).
- Persian is the default language: descriptions and answers are written in Persian
  and spoken with the phone's Persian text-to-speech voice.
- Memory saving: small frames (384–640 px), short prompts (last 4 observations,
  last 1 Q/A), short answers, tensors freed after every run, auto-recovery after
  a Chrome "Aw, Snap!" crash (reloads the model with lower image detail).
- Settings: camera picker (Auto picks a USB camera when one is connected),
  image detail, read aloud, auto-recover.

PHONE SETUP
- Latest Chrome for Android. Page served over https or http://localhost.
- Persian TTS voice: Settings → Text-to-speech → Google engine → install Persian;
  if not offered, install the "sherpa-onnx TTS Engine" app with a Persian Piper
  voice and make it the default engine.
- Offline speech recognition: install the Persian offline speech pack (Google).

HARDWARE – PHASE 1 (wired)
- A small USB (UVC, "driver-free") camera on a 3D-printed clip that slides onto the
  temple arm of normal glasses, behind the hinge, on the outside.
- Thin, flexible USB cable along the temple, behind the ear, down to the phone
  through a USB-C OTG adapter. The phone sits in a pocket.
- Camera choice:
    * Preferred: small UVC board (≈15–32 mm square), 2–5 MP, 70–90° field of view,
      fixed focus set to ~1 m–infinity, ≤10 g. Should be listed as UVC / plug-and-play.
    * Lightest: 7–8 mm cylindrical endoscope camera, BUT most are focused at 3–10 cm;
      only buy one sold as "far focus" or with an adjustable focus.
    * Bought in Iran (Aftab Rayaneh, checked 2026-11): OV2710 1080p UVC board
      (38×38 mm, M12 lens, ~642k Toman, pre-order) or 1 MP XH57 strip (35×12×4.9 mm,
      71°, focus 15–150 cm, pre-order). Both are presets in the .scad (preset = ...).
      Avoid: endoscopes sold in Iran (focus ~2 cm), the 15.6M-Toman OV5640 (overpriced,
      autofocus not needed since frames are only 384–640 px).
- Mount: hardware/glasses_cam_mount.scad (parametric OpenSCAD). Enter the temple arm
  size and camera size, export STL, print in PETG. Camera tilted ~10° down.
- FIRST CHECK: plug the camera into the S24 FE and see if Chrome lists it in
  Settings → Camera (or Device check). Android only shows USB cameras to Chrome when
  the phone's system supports external UVC cameras. If it doesn't show up, use the
  phase 2 fallback below.

HARDWARE – ESP32-CAM BUILD (Arduino, with the physical button, NO pocket hub) – 2026-11-04
Parts (Iran, approx.):
  - ESP32-CAM, preferably with OV2640 (hardware JPEG). A GC2145 / "RHYX-M21-45" camera (e.g. ECA,
    ~2.26M T) also works: the firmware detects it and encodes JPEG in software (~0.3 s slower).
  - Bought in Tehran (checked 2026-11-04): Idea Electronic, Jomhouri St. – ESP32-CAM OV2640
    1,499,000 T + ESP32-CAM-MB shield (micro-USB, CH340G) 272,428 T, both in stock.
  - AI-Thinker ESP32-CAM (OV2640) + ESP32-CAM-MB programmer: ~1.0M Toman as a bundle
    (Jamtronic), or the board alone ~0.6M (Basalam) / ~1.4M (Torob sellers).
  - 6×6×7 mm tactile switch, 220–330 µF 10 V capacitor (6.3×11 mm), thin USB-A cable (1–1.5 m),
    USB-C OTG adapter (the phone powers the camera), 1 small zip-tie. Optional: OV2640 with
    a 75 mm ribbon if the stock ribbon can't bend the lens to the front edge.
  - hardware/pocket_hub.scad is NOT used in this build (kept only as a later option).
Steps:
  1. Flash firmware/openvision_cam/openvision_cam.ino with the ESP32-CAM-MB
     (Arduino IDE, "esp32" by Espressif, board "ESP32 Wrover Module", PSRAM Enabled,
     partition "Minimal SPIFFS (1.9MB APP with OTA)"). Change AP_PASS first.
  2. Bench test: power it, join Wi-Fi "OpenVision-Cam" with the phone, open
     http://192.168.4.1 → a test picture. Fix FLIP_V / MIRROR_H if upside down.
  3. Wire: USB cable red → 5V, black → GND, capacitor across them at the board; switch
     between IO13 and GND. Cut the header pins flush (later updates go over Wi-Fi, OTA).
  4. No calipers needed: glasses_cam_mount.scad defaults (cam_type "esp32cam", mount_style
     "strap") use the Ai-Thinker datasheet size 27×40.5×4.5 mm. Print part="mount" (flat tray,
     no supports) and part="lid". PETG.
     Laser-cut alternative (acrylic / plexi, no printer): hardware/laser/glasses_cam_laser.scad –
     a stack of 2 mm (7 layers) or 3 mm (6 layers) acrylic plates held by 4 × M2 screws + nuts
     (nut traps in frame layer 1). Same straps, same 10° tilt (cut into the layers). Set sheet_t to
     the measured sheet thickness, part="sheet" → export DXF. Order notes: hardware/laser/laserOrder.txt.
  5. Assemble: board flat in the box, ESP32 shield side toward the lid (vents), antenna end at
     the back. Camera ribbon bent forward into the tilted front seat, a drop of CA glue.
     Switch on the block behind the board, plunger through the lid. Zip-tie round the cable
     inside the exit slot (strain relief). Anti-slip pad (1 mm foam / inner-tube rubber) on the
     inner face. Two 150 mm zip-ties (or reusable ties ≤4.8 mm, or ~30 mm silicone O-rings)
     wrap box + temple arm between the guide fins – fits arms ~1.5–8 mm thick, 2–15 mm tall,
     and hold the lid on.
  6. Route the cable along the arm with 3–4 small zip-ties / O-rings, behind the ear, to the phone.
Phone:
  - Open index.html from http://localhost (e.g. Termux: `python -m http.server 8080`) – an
    https site can't talk to the camera's plain http.
  - Wi-Fi → "OpenVision-Cam" → keep connected without internet; turn mobile data off if
    pictures don't arrive. Settings → Camera → "ESP32 Wi-Fi camera" → pill shows
    "connected · button on".
Use: tap = describe, hold (450 ms) = ask; while listening, press again = done speaking.
Image settings: Settings → "Wi-Fi camera image settings" on the page (resolution ≤640×480, quality,
brightness/contrast/saturation, exposure, gain, white balance, effect, lens correction, flip/mirror,
flash LED on capture) with a test picture. Firmware endpoints /settings + /control; values are saved
on the camera (flash) and kept after restart. "Reset to defaults" = the values at the top of the .ino.
Firmware runs the CPU at 160 MHz and the camera clock at 10 MHz (less heat, less drain on the
phone). The box is ~66 × 31 × 13 mm and ~18 g on one arm.

HARDWARE – PHASE 2 (ideas, later)
- Physical button on the glasses: a small Bluetooth HID button sending Space/Enter
  (tap = describe, hold = ask), or a button wired into the camera module.
- Wireless: ESP32-S3 camera board + small LiPo on the glasses, sending JPEG frames
  over Wi-Fi to the page. Removes the cable, adds weight/battery to the glasses,
  and works even if the phone doesn't support USB cameras.
- Bone-conduction or open earbuds for the voice output.
- Native Android app (instead of Chrome) if more memory or speed is needed.
